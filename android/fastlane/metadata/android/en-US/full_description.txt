Family Connect is a private messenger for one family — grandparents to kids — and nobody else. No feed, no strangers, no noise.

It works out of the box: install, pick a username, then create your family — you become its owner — or join one with an invite code. The owner decides whether the code admits people instantly or each request needs approval, and can rotate the code or remove members at any time.

What makes Family Connect different is where your messages live. There is no vendor cloud: every installation talks to a single Family Connect server. Out of the box that is the default server we operate, so you can start right away. But the server software is free and open source (MIT, written in Rust), and the app switches to your own server at any time — a home machine, a spare computer, a small box in a closet ("Use a different server" on the sign-in screen). Point your family's apps at your own hardware and your conversations exist in exactly two places: your server and your family's devices. No company can read, mine, or sell them, because no company has them. One server can host several families, so one technically inclined relative can cover everyone.

Day to day it works like any modern messenger:

• One shared chat for the whole family, plus a private one-to-one chat between any two members
• Photos, videos, voice notes, files and shared locations — up to ten attachments in one message
• One-to-one voice and video calls, peer to peer, through your phone's own call screen
• Replies, edits, emoji reactions and polls
• A shared family board of pinned notes
• Birthdays, so nobody has to remember
• Real-time delivery with typing indicators, read receipts in one-to-one chats and unread counts
• Notifications when messages arrive while the app is closed
• History kept on the device for offline reading
• Nine languages, following your phone's

An optional assistant can answer questions in the family chat when the person running the server turns it on and supplies their own provider key. It is off unless they enable it, and the app tells you what is sent.

What it does not have: ads, analytics, tracking, or profiling of any kind. Two Google services are used and nothing else — Firebase Cloud Messaging, because waking a closed app is something only the platform can do, and Google Maps, only to draw the map on a shared location and only while the map switch in Settings is on. Both can be seen in the source.

Accounts are a username and a password — no email address, no phone number, no contact-list upload, nothing to verify. If you want your family's conversations off big-tech servers and on hardware you own, this is what Family Connect is for.
