# One account in several families (#88)

*Assessment and plan, 2026-10-22. **DECIDED 2026-10-22 — D1–D8 all (a), as recommended:** one account with several
memberships; a family switcher; any member may create a family while owning none (≤ 5 per account, its own operator
switch); invitations are accepted or declined; an existing account may join with a code under the family's rules; one
assistant chat per family; only an owner of ALL the member's families may reset their password; old apps see the
home family only.*

The issue: *"Root Family exists. Somebody from this family got married and can create his own family on this server, and
obtain opportunity to be a member of root family and be an owner of his own family. This family creation is possible
only inside the app. Creation of families via registration should be as it is for now. The owner of the family can
invite members of families to which he belongs. Also in the new Family new members can register with a family invite
code by the family rules."*

## 1. What it means today, and the size of the change

Today an account belongs to **at most one family**: `users.family_id` is a single column, loaded into every request's
`AuthUser`, and every family endpoint is "my family" (`/families/mine`, `/families/leave`, the owner's endpoints, the
board, the sticker pack, statistics). Every client mirrors that: one `family` value (iOS `AppSession.family`, Android's
DataStore `familyStatus`/`familyName`, web `Store.family`, Windows `SessionState.Family`), one local cache per install
with **no family id on any row**, one board cursor and one pack cursor, and a "family gate" that decides between
create/join/pending/chats from that one value. The board, pack, `chat_updated` and `chat_removed` frames carry no
family id; `member_joined`/`member_left`/`family_owner` carry one that every client ignores.

So this is not a feature added to the family; it changes what an account IS. It is the largest change since v1 —
larger than #107 (§9).

What is already account-level and stays so: the username, password, display name, profile picture and birthday; blocks
(`member_blocks` is a pair, not a membership); the assistant consents; devices and sessions.
What is family-level and stays so: the family chat, groups, the board, the sticker pack, the owner's settings
(join policy, cap, language, the assistant switches, greeting places), join requests, reports, statistics.

## 2. The model — **D1**

**Recommended (a): one account, several memberships.** A new table `family_members (family_id, user_id, joined_at)`
replaces `users.family_id`. The same person, with one login, is in the parents' family and owns their own. Everything
family-level gets its family from the CHAT or the URL, never from the account.

Alternative (b): a separate account per family, and the apps learn to hold several accounts on one server with an
account switcher. Cheaper on the server, but every client becomes a multi-session app, the person has two passwords,
two profiles, two pictures, and "invite members of families to which he belongs" becomes "invite their other
account" — which is not what the issue describes.

## 3. How the apps show several families — **D2**

**Recommended (a): one family at a time, with a switcher.** The chat list, the board, stickers, the roster, the owner's
settings and statistics show the CURRENT family. The switcher (the family's name at the top of the chat list; a menu on
Mac, web and Windows) lists every family with its unread count, so nothing is missed. Pushes and notifications arrive
from every family, titled with that family's name when the person is in more than one, and tapping one opens it in its
family. Direct chats with somebody (§6) appear in every family both of you are in.

Alternative (b): one merged chat list with each chat labelled by its family, and a family picker only on the board,
stickers and settings. Fewer taps, but two "Family" chats in one list, two boards behind one tab, and every screen has
to say whose it is.

A person in ONE family sees exactly what they see today: no switcher, no family names in titles.

## 4. Creating another family — **D3**

"Only inside the app" — a member creates a family from the switcher's "New Family…", never through registration, and
registration stays as it is.

**Recommended (a):** any member may create one, and the creator becomes its owner with a family chat, exactly as
`POST /families` does today. Limits: an account may belong to at most **5** families (operator setting
`[families] max_families_per_account`), and may CREATE a family only while it owns none — so the married son owns his
one family; a second needs the first handed on or deleted. In-app creation is its own operator switch,
`[families] member_families`, separate from `registration`: a server closed to strangers' families
(`registration = false`) can still let its members start theirs with `member_families = true`, which is the issue's
case. Left unset it follows `registration` (amended in review): on by default, but a server an operator closed is not
reopened by an upgrade that adds a door handing out new invite codes.

Alternatives: **(b)** in-app creation obeys `registration` too (a closed server closes it); **(c)** no "own none" rule
— only the per-account cap.

## 5. Inviting — **D4**

"The owner can invite members of families to which he belongs." The owner picks people from the families they share
with them (the members of their other families; never anyone else on the server).

**Recommended (a): an invitation the invited person accepts.** They see "Anna invited you to "Ivanovs"" with Accept and
Decline, in the app and by push; until they accept, they are not in the family and nobody there sees them. Nobody is
ever put into a family they did not agree to — which matters because a family's owner has powers over its members
(§7), and anyone may create a family (§4).

Alternative (b): added at once, like a group (#107), and they may leave.

**The invite code** stays the door for new people, by the family's rules (open, approval, closed), exactly as today
at registration. **D5 — may an EXISTING account also enter a code** ("Join Another Family…" in the switcher)?
Recommended **yes**, under the same rules: the bride's family may live on the same server with nobody shared, and a
code is how the owner already says "you may come in". Alternative: no — existing accounts enter only by invitation.

## 6. Direct chats and the assistant's chat

- **Direct chats: one per pair, whatever they share.** The server already allows only one direct chat per pair. Two
  people in two shared families keep ONE conversation, shown in both families; it closes (as today) only when they share
  no family any more. Proposed, not a question.
- **The assistant's private chat — D6.** **Recommended (a): one per family**, following that family's owner's switches
  (pictures, recent history, lookups, language) — it is "this family's assistant", reached from that family's list.
  Alternative (b): one per account, under the switches of the family the account joined first. (b) is one chat instead
  of several, but which owner's rules apply would be a rule the member cannot see.

## 7. An owner's power over a member in several families — **D7**

Today an owner can **reset a member's password** (`POST /families/members/{id}/password`), signing them out everywhere.
With several families, the owner of any one of them would hold the keys to the person's whole account — every family,
every direct chat. **Recommended (a): an owner may reset the password only of a member whose every family that owner
owns.** A child only in the parents' family: the parents reset it, as today. The married son, in the parents' family
and owning his own: nobody can reset his password but himself. Alternatives: **(b)** the owner of the family the member
joined first; **(c)** any of their owners (today's rule, carried over).

The rest is per family and needs no decision: removing a member removes them from THAT family (its chat, its groups;
direct chats close only with peers they no longer share any family with); reports go to the owner of the family the
message was in; the owner's settings, invite code, join requests and statistics are that family's. Setting a member's
birthday stays with any owner of a family they are in — the birthday is one fact, shown in each.

**Leaving** one family leaves that family only. An account becomes "without a family" (and the 7-day sweep's clock
starts) only when it leaves its LAST one. **Deleting an account** leaves every family, each with its owner hand-off.

## 8. Apps that predate this — **D8**

The protocol has no capability handshake, and an old app shown two families would mix two boards, two packs and two
family chats into one. **Recommended (a): the "home family".** A client that does not send the new header
`X-FC-Families: 1` (and the same as a WebSocket query parameter) is answered as today, about the family the account
joined FIRST: `/me`, `/families/mine`, `GET /chats`, the board, the pack and every frame are scoped to it, and nothing
from the other families reaches it. Updating the app shows the rest. Alternative (b): the server refuses an old app
for an account in two or more families, with a "please update" error.

## 9. On the wire (sketch, to be settled in docs/protocol.md after the decisions)

- `GET /me` gains `families: [{id, name, role, unread}]` and `pending_join_requests: […]`; `family` stays (the home
  family) for old clients.
- `GET /families/{id}` replaces `/families/mine` (which stays, meaning the home family); every owner and member
  endpoint gains a `/families/{id}/…` form; the board and pack move to `/families/{id}/board…` and `/families/{id}/pack…`.
- `POST /families` (in-app, while in a family) → `201`; new errors `too_many_families`, `already_own_a_family`,
  `member_families_disabled`.
- Invitations: `POST /families/{id}/invitations {user_id}`, `GET /me/invitations`, `POST /invitations/{id}/accept`,
  `…/decline`; frames `invitation` / `invitation_removed`.
- `Chat`, `Note`, `PackItem` gain `family_id` (a direct chat carries the family it was opened in, and is listed in
  every shared family); `board_note`, `pack_item`, `chat_updated`, `chat_removed` thereby say whose they are; a new
  `family_added` / `family_removed` pair tells a person's devices about a family they joined, created or left.
- Migration: `family_members` filled from `users.family_id`; the AI chat's uniqueness becomes per (account, family);
  `users.deleted_family_id` becomes a list; per-family board and pack cursors on every client.

### Clients (all six)

Each one: a family list instead of one family; the switcher; "New Family…", "Join Another Family…", the invitation
banner and the owner's "Invite…" (members of their other families); the cache keyed by family (a new column on chats,
notes, pack items and the roster; schema migrations on iOS SwiftData, Android Room 34→35, Windows schema 11; the web
keeps memory only); a board and pack cursor per family; the gate decides from the family LIST; push and notification
routing by `family_id`; and the six "find THE family chat" places (pin order, the list title) become "the current
family's chat". Every new string in all nine languages.
